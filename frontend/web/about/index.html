Laafi Ma: health within every woman's reach

An open-source, voice-first app for women and girls in Burkina Faso. It speaks Mooré, Dioula and French, works for women who cannot read, and keeps working offline.

Open the live demo Source code (MIT) Public statistics

The problem

Many rural women in Burkina Faso speak only Mooré or Dioula and cannot read French, the language of most health apps. Care during pregnancy and family planning are free, but the information does not reach women in their language, at the right time. Paper health records get lost, and a woman facing violence often cannot call for help in front of others.

What Laafi Ma does

Voice health assistant

A woman asks aloud in her language. Answers come only from a vetted base; danger signs send her to care.

Private health record

Cycle, pregnancy, children's vaccines, prescriptions and reminders, encrypted on her phone.

Help and protection

Discreet help request, directory of services, calculator disguise and quick exit.

Health worker space

Offline tool for community health workers: follow-up, decision aid, anonymous referrals.

Learn

Short audio lessons for girls, mothers, men and families, with a quiz and no grades.

Safety by design

  • Health data stays on the woman's phone, encrypted. The server only gets anonymous counts.
  • Public statistics hide any number under 10, so no woman can be identified.
  • The assistant never diagnoses or prescribes. It does not generate free medical text.

Where we are

  • Working demo online: app, health worker space, admin console and public statistics.
  • Open-source voice engine: speech recognition, translation and speech synthesis in Mooré, Dioula and French.
  • Next: validation of all health content by a midwife, review of Mooré and Dioula by native speakers, and a first pilot with women and health workers.

The demo uses public WHO and UNFPA guidance that a midwife has not reviewed yet, and the app says so. It holds no real patient data.

Version française

Laafi Ma est une application de santé et de protection pour les femmes et les filles du Burkina Faso, à la voix, en mooré, en dioula et en français. Elle réunit un assistant santé vocal, un carnet de santé sur le téléphone, une aide discrète contre les violences, un espace pour les agents de santé et des capsules audio. Le code est libre, sous licence MIT.
